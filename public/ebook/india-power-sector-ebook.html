1 Industry Overview

India is the 3rd largest electricity producer and 3rd largest consumer globally. The sector has undergone a 22x transformation — from 20 GW in 1975 to 450+ GW in 2025, powering 1.4 billion people across 36 states and union territories.

Key Achievements

  • World's 3rd largest power system after China and USA
  • Achieved 100% village electrification (Saubhagya scheme)
  • Solar grew from 0 to 90 GW in just 12 years (2012–2025)
  • Crossed 500 GW total generation capacity milestone in 2026
  • Committed to Net Zero by 2070 and 500 GW non-fossil by 2030
💡 Key Insight: India adds more power capacity per year (~40-50 GW) than most countries have in total. The energy transition is accelerating — 90% of new additions are now renewable.

Solar — The Star Performer

Solar has grown from nearly zero in 2010 to 90.6 GW by 2025. India is now the 4th largest solar market globally. Tariffs dropped 88% — from ₹17/kWh in 2010 to ₹1.99/kWh in 2024, making solar cheaper than new coal plants.

Wind — The Pioneer

India was an early wind adopter (1990s). With 47.6 GW, it's the 4th largest wind market. Turbines evolved from 250 kW to 5.5 MW. Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, and Rajasthan dominate. Offshore wind (30 GW target) is the next frontier.

💡 Did You Know? India's solar capacity grew 45x in just 10 years (2015–2025). At current growth rate, solar alone will cross 280 GW by 2030 — more than India's entire coal fleet today.

The Capacity vs Generation Gap

This is the most important concept in power sector analysis. Coal has 47% of capacity but produces 67% of electricity because it runs at 64% PLF. Solar has 20% of capacity but only 8% of generation because CUF is just 18% (daylight constraint).

💡 Why Coal Still Matters: Even as India adds 50+ GW of solar/wind annually, coal remains indispensable for nighttime/cloudy base load. Battery Energy Storage (BESS) at scale is the only way to displace coal — India targets 5 GW BESS by 2027.

Key Ownership Insights

  • Private sector owns 79% of solar and 91% of wind — driving India's clean energy transition
  • NTPC (Central PSU) is the single largest generator at 73 GW — larger than many countries
  • Nuclear is exclusively Central PSU (government) — 8.18 GW via NPCIL
  • State PSU remains the most fossil-dependent at 68% thermal
  • Private sector crossed 50% non-fossil for the first time in 2024
💡 Transition Story: In 1991, India opened electricity to private investment. Today, private companies own more than both PSU categories combined. AGEL (Adani Green) alone has 20 GW — more than many state utilities.

9.4 Best Practices by Segment

Coal: Predictive maintenance (AI/ML), NDT every 2 years after 15-year mark, coal quality analyzers, boiler water chemistry control, fire suppression barriers.

Wind: Condition Monitoring Systems (CMS), IEC 62305 Lightning Protection, blade inspection (drone/rope), structural health monitoring, cyclone stow protocols.

Solar: Quarterly drone thermal imaging, string-level IV curve testing, tracker actuator maintenance, hail-resistant module procurement, inverter N+1 redundancy.

Key Insurance Insights

  • Business Interruption claims often 2-3x larger than Material Damage claims
  • Wind turbine crane availability in India: 3-6 months wait — extends BI significantly
  • Solar hailstorm emerged as #1 peril post-2020 (can damage 80% of modules in minutes)
  • Coal plant boiler failures have longest lead time: 12-18 months for turbine rotor replacement
  • Pre-agreed repair protocols in policy wording reduce claims settlement time by 60%
💡 The Opportunity: With India adding ₹5-6 lakh crore of power assets annually (2025–2030), the construction + operational insurance premium pool will double within 5 years. Specialized underwriting for BESS, offshore wind, and green H2 represents a first-mover advantage.
